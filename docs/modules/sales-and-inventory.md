# Sales and inventory

## Ownership and source of truth

[useInventory](../../src/hooks/useInventory.ts) reads item balances and movement history; item creation registers zero-stock SKUs. The `items_unique_sku` key is `(tenant_id, brand_id, size_kg, mouth_size)`. Brand identity is required and tenant-qualified; names come from `lpg_brands`, and `size_kg` is the sole stored weight. Catalog forms, POS and inventory share the same polled inventory query. [POS](../../src/pages/POS.tsx) owns the cart, and [usePOS](../../src/hooks/usePOS.ts) calls `create_pos_sale`.

The current bottle/charge extension is defined in the [bottle receivables migration](../../supabase/migrations/20261023012148_sales_bottle_receivables.sql), building on the payment contract in the [multi-wallet migration](../../supabase/migrations/20261020155555_multi_wallet_finance.sql), extending the idempotent checkout. The base checkout, stock recorder and movement-trigger definitions are in the [authorization migration](../../supabase/migrations/20261004192158_tenant_permission_authorization.sql); the sale-tier migration below extends checkout. The [baseline](../../supabase/migrations/20260627000000_cloud_baseline.sql) creates `trg_update_item_inventory`, an AFTER INSERT trigger on `inventory_movements`; later SQL moves/redefines its function in `app_private`.

## Customer and price rules

- A sale requires a customer phone. With `p_customer_id`, SQL loads the saved tenant customer and leaves their details unchanged. Without an ID, an already-saved exact phone is rejected with instructions to select that customer; otherwise the customer is created inside checkout, with a blank name becoming `Unknown`.
- [bdPhone.ts](../../src/lib/bdPhone.ts) and the [final customer-phone migration](../../supabase/migrations/20260702155154_numeric_customer_phone_lengths.sql) accept `01[3-9]` followed by eight digits or the same number prefixed by `088`. Search considers both representations, but exact stored strings are tenant-unique; they are not canonicalized to one identity. Preserve this distinction when changing lookup/deduplication.
- [POSItemsGrid](../../src/components/pos/POSItemsGrid.tsx) uses [getSellingPrice](../../src/lib/productPricing.ts) to select the latest active price-book row by item/tier/line type, respecting inclusive `effective_from` and exclusive `effective_to`. Missing wholesale prices fall back to retail. A missing active price disables adding that sale type and blocks checkout; an explicitly saved zero price remains valid. Switching the draft's retail/wholesale control (before a saved customer is selected) or selecting a customer with another tier reprices every existing cart line, retaining quantities, discount, exchange cost and notes. Empty returns remain zero. Price-book refreshes alone do not rewrite cart prices.
- The [sale tier migration](../../supabase/migrations/20261019031721_sale_pricing_tier.sql) adds required `sales.sale_tier`, restricted to `retail` or `wholesale`. The [customer tier lock migration](../../supabase/migrations/20261022123445_pos_customer_tier_lock.sql) requires new sales to match the selected customer's saved tier. POS disables both pricing buttons while a saved customer is selected; clearing the customer restores draft tier choice. SQL locks the tenant-qualified customer row against edits and rejects mismatches before any sale, stock or accounting writes. Completed request retries return their original result even after customer reclassification. History, sale details and reprinted receipts use the saved sale tier. Legacy sales are inferred once from the same-tenant customer's current tier, defaulting to retail for unsupported values; their original historical tier cannot be reconstructed. Apply this migration before deploying the dependent frontend.
- SQL independently resolves the active price at checkout request time using the same effective windows and wholesale-to-retail fallback, and rejects a submitted price mismatch. Empty returns must have a zero price. No arbitrary unit-price override capability is implemented; new customer drafts can choose retail/wholesale before saving or checkout. Refresh prices explicitly reloads and reprices the draft for review; background reads do not silently change it.

## Product details and pricing

Inventory keeps four compact summary boxes inside one toolbar: total units, low stock, out of stock and catalog. They form one desktop row and two phone columns. The complete brand-ordered catalog scrolls without pagination; summary totals and product selection use the full collection.

The [inventory migration](../../supabase/migrations/20261018101202_inventory_product_details.sql) is required for the redesigned product dialog. `price_books.price` remains the selling price; nullable `mrp` is reference information only and never enters cart totals, discounts or receipt data. Receipts show the saved `unit_price` as the per-unit amount. Existing prices are preserved with no invented MRP; the price-book key includes line type so refill/package can share an effective timestamp.

The [owner/commission/brand-order migration](../../supabase/migrations/20261018181700_inventory_owner_commission_brand_order.sql) additionally requires trusted OWNER membership for `save_product_prices` and `set_product_commissions`, alongside `inventory:manage`. Managers retain ordinary inventory management but cannot change prices or commission. The product RPC returns `can_manage_prices` for UI controls; no role name or user metadata is trusted in the browser. Price saves lock the exact item and atomically version both prices for the selected retail/wholesale tier, check loaded effective timestamps, close old active windows and preserve future scheduled prices. Drafts survive polling and failed saves; explicit reload actions discard the draft. Successful saves stay in the dialog. Price saves alone leave cart lines and saved sales at their captured prices; switching the draft sale tier explicitly reprices its cart.

`get_product_inventory` requires `inventory:view` or `inventory:manage` and returns only the exact item's current pricing/commission, estimated buying costs and weekly quantities. Commission has separate manually set refill and package per-unit rates, independent of retail/wholesale pricing. Both rates save atomically in one attributed history row. Every set, including setting the same value again, appends an attributed `product_commission_rates` row. Item locks and expected rate IDs reject conflicting saves. Commission history stores required `refill_commission_per_unit` and `package_commission_per_unit`. The canonical development migration materializes old shared values once, preserving rate identity, actor and date. Only `set_product_commissions` writes rates; the single-rate RPC is removed. History UPDATE/DELETE is blocked; RLS and no browser table grants keep history out of the frontend. Neither commission nor MRP enters POS/receipt calculations. Price saves invalidate inventory/POS pricing; commission saves invalidate inventory only. The product dialog shows commission only in each buying-cost breakdown. Owners open a separate commission popup beneath those estimates; cancelling or closing it preserves the product dialog, and successful saves refresh the estimates.

Balances and overview use the same polled query. Low (1–5 units) and out-of-stock (zero) product counts are independent for filled/empty stocks. A fourth box shows total registered items and active catalog brands, including active brands without items. Add Item remains available to inventory managers. Products are grouped by brand identity and ascending weight, then mouth size for ties; inactive brands with products stay visible. Settings' LPG Brands arrows persist the full tenant brand order with stale-list checks, including inactive brands. New brands append to that order. All brand selectors use the same ordered list RPC; inactive filtering keeps the relative order. Reordering requires `settings:manage` and invalidates brand/inventory queries.

For each purchase type, the buying estimate selects the latest **Received**, non-cancelled purchase containing the exact `item_id` (including its mouth size). It divides that product/type's billed line total by received units and adds `(transport + labour) / all units received in that purchase`. Purchase commission is ignored. The current manually set refill or package commission per unit is deducted from the corresponding estimate, so the formula is billed bottle cost + shared transport/labour per unit - manual commission per unit. Historical billed/received discrepancies therefore affect the estimate. Refill/package can come from different receipts; dates and the cost breakdown are shown. This uses current saved purchase financials and does not include extra transit operating expenses or pretend to be inventory valuation.

Eight entries, including zero-activity weeks, run Saturday 00:00 through the following Saturday exclusively in `Asia/Dhaka`; the displayed end date is Friday. Shared column headings group sold refill/package as negative quantities and incoming empties/package/refill as positive quantities; zero activity stays neutral. Completed sale headers supply dates and line types for sold refill/package counts. Empty returns use actual receipt product IDs and receipt dates. Development normalization records earlier checkout returns at their original sale dates without inserting stock movements. Received purchase headers supply receipt dates and actual received quantities for bought refill/package; cancelled and unreceived purchases are excluded. These are activity summaries, not opening/closing balances or all manual adjustments. The separate Movement History tab retains the stock ledger.

## Atomic checkout

`create_pos_sale` requires `pos:sell`, a nonempty JSON array, supported line types, positive integer quantities, finite, nonnegative prices/discount/exchange cost with at most two decimal places, an active book price for sold products, a discount no greater than subtotal, and a representable nonnegative final total. Total refill quantity must equal total empty-return quantity **across the cart**, not per SKU/brand/mouth size.

| Line type, quantity `q` | Filled delta | Empty delta |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `refill` | `-q` | `0` |
| `package` | `-q` | `-q` |
| Expected `empty_return` line | `0` | `0`; creates the product obligation |
| Physical receipt allocation, actual quantity `q` | `0` | `+q` on the actual received product |

The package empty deduction is implemented behavior; do not silently replace it with an assumed cylinder model. The UI checks filled availability, while SQL locks every tenant item in SKU order, validates aggregate filled/empty deltas across the whole cart, applies movement deltas through the trigger, and rejects negative final balances. A late failure rolls back the customer insertion, sale, lines, movements and accounting together. Never update item balances again alongside movement insertion.

The transaction stores a `completed` sale and line totals, calculates `subtotal - discount + exchange cost`, posts one negative customer invoice charge and positive receipts only for money received, and credits the selected wallets through their ledger entries. Default payment is full Cash Register; split/partial and zero initial payments preserve customer receivables. Zero-total sales create no payment entries. Historical ledger gaps are represented by the migration baseline without inventing old income; see [Finance](finance.md).

Checkout requires `p_request_id`. A tenant-scoped unique key and transaction advisory lock serialize concurrent matching requests. The saved request includes its actor and exact payload; changed payloads are rejected, while matching retries return the original response even if prices, customer details or stock changed later. Permission and active membership are checked before replay. Failure rolls back the request reservation with all other effects, so a corrected retry can succeed. A request ID is mandatory.

The existing UI ref lock prevents overlapping clicks. Before sending, the hook stores the request ID and payload in session storage scoped to tenant/user. Explicit input/constraint rejection releases it; lost responses, expired sessions, gateway errors and transient database failures retain it across reloads because an earlier attempt may have committed. Recover checkout replays the original request without automatic printing and clears the current draft after confirmation. A confirmed new sale gets a new ID even when its contents match the preceding sale. Partial/zero monetary payments and outstanding bottle obligations are supported; no completed-sale cancellation/refund API is introduced. Closing an unsubmitted confirmation has no stock/accounting effect; an uncertain submitted request must be recovered.

## Commit, refresh and receipt handoff

POS uses the shared page scroller and [adaptive grid profiles](adaptive-layout.md). Product columns follow their actual container with compact 14rem minimum/17.5rem maximum cards, without a fixed desktop ceiling. Checkout stacks with shortcuts when workspace width is at most 52rem; wider workspaces retain the sticky 280px checkout panel and bounded viewport height. Phone cards can use the compact workspace width. Prices omit redundant zero decimals while retaining fractional values. Discount and exchange cost keep direct decimal entry without spinner/wheel stepping. Cart lines scroll together without pagination; short windows scroll the bounded checkout body. Inventory, POS and product selectors start each brand on a new row in Settings order; picker pages account for these row boundaries without losing records or selections. Shared payment editors use left-aligned plain decimals with natural cursor/selection behavior. Browser fixtures cover compact cards, sidebar states, dialogs, touch, short windows and display scaling; physical-device acceptance remains separate.

[completeSale](../../src/lib/completeSale.ts) waits for checkout, commits UI success/clears the cart, then optionally prints. Printing failure returns separately and never reruns checkout. [useReceiptPrinter](../../src/hooks/useReceiptPrinter.ts) reloads the completed sale and latest tenant configuration for checkout printing and reprints. Amounts/lines are saved, but names/brand/business identity come from current related records rather than a full receipt snapshot. Device matching and persistent print-request deduplication are documented in the [printing guide](../../print-bridge/README.md).

Checkout and reprint controls offer browser printing alongside the configured USB queue. The method is remembered per tenant in this browser and does not replace shared USB settings. Browser printing needs no bridge or saved USB configuration; it uses the same receipt formatting and reads the committed sale. Cancelling the browser dialog leaves the sale saved. USB failures never trigger an automatic browser fallback because a job may already have printed.

Sale success invalidates inventory, customers, logistics/wallets and Dashboard families, plus Finance and the sales/movements prefixes in `usePOS`. Keep downstream refreshes when modifying accounting or receipt data. Inventory balances/history also poll; Dashboard totals/feed use browser-local day boundaries as described in [architecture](../architecture.md#workspace-lifecycle-and-cache-behavior).

## Inventory history

The Inventory History tab uses [useInventoryHistory](../../src/hooks/useInventory.ts) to apply purchase/sale association and selected product IDs to the tenant-scoped movement query before ordering and pagination. It reads 10 entries plus one look-ahead row; changing filters resets the page. No selected products means no entries; Select all removes the product restriction. The picker reuses stock-balance tiles, saved brand order and ascending weights. It is also shared with Customers, where stock counts are hidden and a capability-gated identity-only catalog is used. Search and pages preserve staged selection across the complete catalog. Closing the picker discards only its un-applied selection.

[Movement descriptions](../../src/lib/inventoryMovement.ts) distinguish sold filled cylinders, returned empties, purchase dispatch/receipts, and cancellation reversals from the recorded reference and deltas. Full references link to the shared transaction detail modal or existing purchase dialogs. The transaction view is shared with customer history; its Print receipt action opens the shared receipt dialog, and long saved line lists are paginated with whole-sale totals. Old exact purchase-note references are migrated once to tenant-qualified `reference_id`/`reference_type`; runtime readers never parse notes as relationships. Unclassifiable historical receipts use `purchase_history` without inventing a refill/package allocation. Source names come from authorized sale reads and the bounded purchase-by-ID RPC for referenced purchases. Source-detail controls require their existing sale/purchase permissions; inventory-only access does not grant transaction access. Names reflect current related records, as do existing receipts.

Purchase and inventory filter bars share a slate surface in [controls.css](../../src/components/ui/controls.css), preserving native fixed-choice selects and shared date controls.

Manual adjustment is available through `create_inventory_movement`, gated by `inventory:manage`, but has no current UI dialog. The private recorder locks the item and rejects negative resulting stock; purchase workflows use it under their own gate. Browser callers cannot update/delete movements or set balances directly. There is no implemented sale void/refund/reversal flow.

## Wallet payments and customer dues

Checkout omits `p_payments` (or sends null) for full Cash Register payment. An explicit array supports up to 20 positive, finite, two-decimal entries across active payment wallets; `[]` means no initial payment. SQL rejects totals above the invoice. Each new sale posts one invoice charge and only the received payment receipts, with matching wallet entries in the same transaction as stock and sale creation. Customer outstanding is the negative customer-ledger sum. Finance and saved-sale details collect against an exact unpaid sale under `finance:manage`. See [Finance](finance.md) for locking, migration, retry and correction rules.

Receipt previews and both print transports share width-specific formatting. Saved receipts read persisted payment attribution and current settlement; later collections are labeled separately, while `paid_at_checkout` retains the original paid amount and checkout attribution is read from immutable `sale_payments`. `unattributed_paid` explicitly reports paid amounts without payment-method evidence; no wallet credits are invented.


## Expected bottles, physical receipts and aggregate settlement

Original immutable `empty_return` sale lines are the sole product agreement. Physical receipts use `sale_bottle_receipts` and `sale_bottle_receipt_lines`; no second obligation table, tracking flag or expected-to-actual allocation is retained. `20261026070000_canonical_development_model.sql` converts earlier checkout returns once into the same model without new stock postings. Settlement uses total expected minus total received. A sale expecting 3 A + 2 B is bottle settled after receiving 5 A, including across multiple receipts. Product differences are informational, never independent outstanding debts. Monetary settlement remains independent.

The Received Bottles checkout editor opens collapsed, with received/expected and outstanding totals. Expected product steppers default to the selected quantities; zero and partial receipt need no note. Other received products opens the existing searchable catalog picker. Later collections start at zero in the compact continuous grid using Settings order, brand/weight/mouth search and measured whole-row pagination (two to four rows according to window height). Selections and acknowledgements persist across search/pages. Unexpected products and cumulative receipts above an original product quantity require acknowledgement. All actual quantities, including aggregate over-receipts, are saved without capping. Notes are optional (1,000 characters); duplicates, fractional/negative quantities and collections without bottles are rejected. Once fulfilled, further collections are disabled while uncertain-request recovery remains available.

`app_private.receive_sale_bottles` remains the only recorder. Immutable headers retain phase, request, time, operator, note and later customer charge in the existing `fee` field. Lines retain actual product IDs, quantities and acknowledgements. The abandoned allocation columns are removed; actual product identity, quantities and acknowledgements remain. Recorded business expenses retain their accounting evidence; new collections never allocate actual products to expectations or debit a wallet. Normalized checkout returns create no new stock, bottle dues or payment attribution. Each sale clamps its outstanding quantity at zero before directory/customer/overview aggregation, preventing excess receipt on one sale from reducing another sale's obligation.

Additional Settlement Cost requires `finance:manage` alongside bottle collection permission. It reuses `sales.additional_charges` and the customer invoice ledger, increasing current payable/due without changing original sale amounts. The existing Collect payment action supports split and partial payments independently of bottles. The receipt, actual-product inventory movement, customer charge and `finance_requests` result commit together under tenant/request and sale locks. Actual items lock in UUID order. Completed request retries return their original result; new requests accept only the canonical fields and reject unsupported payloads. See [Finance](finance.md) for accounting boundaries.

The shared transaction modal shows separate expected/received lists, quantity-based settlement and attributed immutable history; historical expenses remain labeled Business expense. Mutations invalidate Sales, Inventory, Customers, Finance, Dashboard and wallets. Cart/receipt presentation remains package → refill → empty return, in Settings brand order. `get_sale_receipt` loads current business/customer/product details, original sale quantities/prices/discounts and current settlement in one statement snapshot. `useSaleReceipt` and printing share one loader and `buildSaleReceipt`; no receipt snapshot is stored. Preview refetches on each open, polls, and offers Refresh receipt. Printing reloads current saved data again without creating a sale.

Both widths and output methods use the same formatter. Empty rows aggregate original expected quantities by item ID and every actual checkout/later receipt by actual item ID, preserving brand, size and mouth identity. Unexpected products append after original empty entries with Expected 0; actual counts are never capped. Bottle Settled appears when the aggregate obligation is fulfilled, independently of money status. Original Exchange Cost, Additional Settlement Cost, current payable, payment methods, paid and due remain distinct. Restart an older running print bridge to use the updated formatter and over-receipt validator; physical printing is a separate acceptance step.

Weekly Inventory history continues to use actual receipt product IDs and receipt dates; normalized earlier checkout returns retain their original products, quantities and dates. Refill-to-expected-empty parity, package stock deductions, active prices and checkout payment snapshots remain unchanged. Product identity is preserved even when its actual received count exceeds the expected quantity.

`bottles.sql` tests aggregate totals, acknowledgements, multiple/unexpected products, optional notes, zero/default/partial checkout, immutable evidence, settlement-cost idempotency/rollback, independent payment and tenant isolation. Disposable PostgreSQL races duplicate/competing receipts and customer charges alongside payment settlement. Migration preservation tests cover both pre-tracking sales and tracked historical receipts. `bottles-api.mjs --azure --browser` checks real Auth/API and published browser flows only on cleaned-up fixture businesses. `tests/bottles.browser.cjs` uses intercepted APIs for response-loss recovery and 320–1440px layouts. Physical printing, provider settlement and installed-device behavior remain separate acceptance boundaries.
